# The World Without Adults: A Crisis of Global Leadership

There are moments in history when the world feels like it is being run not by statesmen but by rival warlords with nuclear arsenals. The present moment is one such time. From Gaza to Kyiv to Tehran, conflicts rage not because peace is impossible, but because leadership is absent. What we are witnessing today is not merely a series of wars. It is a profound vacuum in global leadership.

For decades after World War II, the world at least pretended to believe in institutions, diplomacy, and the fragile architecture of international law. Today, that façade is crumbling.

## Gaza: When Humanity Became Collateral Damage

The Israel–Palestine conflict has long been one of the most tragic failures of international diplomacy. Yet the current phase has exposed something darker: the moral bankruptcy of global leadership.

Thousands of civilians continue to pay the price for the political paralysis of the international community. Powerful nations issue statements, convene emergency meetings, and then quietly return to their strategic calculations. The United Nations calls for restraint, but its voice has become increasingly ceremonial in a world where military power speaks louder than international law.

The tragedy is not only the violence itself but the absence of a credible mediator. There is no Nelson Mandela figure, no global coalition with moral authority capable of forcing both sides toward a durable peace. Instead, the conflict has become another theatre where geopolitical loyalties dictate morality.

## Ukraine: War as Geopolitical Chess

The Russia–Ukraine war tells a similar story. What began as a brutal invasion quickly turned into a proxy war between global power blocs.

For Moscow, Ukraine is a strategic buffer. For NATO and the West, it is a frontline in the defense of the international order. Between these narratives lies a devastated country whose cities have been reduced to rubble.

The world's most powerful nations are not searching for peace. They are calculating leverage.

Sanctions, weapons shipments, and diplomatic ultimatums have replaced genuine negotiations. The result is a war that drags on indefinitely, feeding military industries and geopolitical ambitions while millions of Ukrainians remain displaced.

In another era, global leaders would have convened tirelessly to impose a ceasefire. Today, the war continues because it suits the strategic interests of those with the power to end it.

## The Expanding Fire: U.S., Israel and Iran

Now the Middle East stands on the edge of an even larger conflagration.

The escalating confrontation between the United States, Israel, and Iran has already destabilized global energy markets and triggered a wider regional crisis. The joint U.S.–Israeli strikes on Iran and Tehran's retaliatory attacks have pushed the region toward open war and disrupted global oil supplies through the Strait of Hormuz, which carries about 20% of the world's oil trade.

The conflict has also produced severe humanitarian consequences and displaced populations across the region.

Yet again, the international community appears powerless, or unwilling, to stop the spiral. Even European leaders have begun warning that unilateral military actions are becoming a dangerous norm in global politics.

The Middle East has always been a powder keg, but today there is no firefighter, only arsonists blaming each other for the flames.

## The Collapse of Moral Authority

What binds these conflicts together is not ideology, geography, or religion. It is the collapse of moral authority in global politics.

The United States once claimed to be the guardian of the liberal world order. Russia claims to challenge Western hegemony. China speaks the language of stability and development. Europe speaks of international law.

But when war erupts, each power interprets justice through the lens of its own strategic interests.

Human rights become selective. International law becomes negotiable. Peace becomes conditional.

## A Leaderless World

The truth is uncomfortable but unavoidable: the world today lacks leaders with the courage to prioritize peace over power.

Global institutions are weaker than ever. Diplomacy has been replaced by televised outrage. Strategic alliances have replaced universal principles.

The world does not lack powerful leaders. It lacks responsible ones.

History often reminds us that wars do not begin because peace is impossible. They begin because no one powerful enough chooses to stop them.

At this moment, humanity is paying the price for that choice.
